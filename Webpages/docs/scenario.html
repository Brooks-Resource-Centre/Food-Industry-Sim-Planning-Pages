3The setting

Describe where the student is standing and what they can walk around.

4What is wrong? (the faults to find)

List each problem you want students to discover. Aim for around 4 to 8. For each one say where it is, what the student would see, how serious it is, and why it matters.

How serious? Use the BRCGS levels:
  • Critical a failure on food safety or a legal issue (certificate refused or suspended).
  • Major a substantial failure to meet a requirement, or serious doubt about the product.
  • Minor a requirement is not fully met but the product is not in doubt.

What is BRCGS? Brand Reputation Compliance Global Standards (formerly the British Retail Consortium Global Standards). It is a widely used food safety certification scheme. An independent auditor inspects a site, reviews its records and grades any problems as critical, major or minor. Retailers often require their suppliers to hold it.

5Records students can look at

Paperwork, logs or labels students can open to check what they have found. Say what each one shows.

6Assets to build

An asset is anything that has to be created to make the scenario: a room, an object, a mark or damage, a document, a person or a sound. List what the scenario needs so the team can plan what can be shared between scenarios and what must be built new. See the Resources page for examples and the common resources.

7Things that look wrong but are fine (optional)

A good example next to a bad one helps students learn to tell the difference.
